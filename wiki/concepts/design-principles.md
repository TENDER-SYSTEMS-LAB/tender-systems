# Design Principles

## The layer the user asked for

After watching two videos on Dieter Rams, the user asked for a set of TENDER SYSTEMS design principles in the manner of Rams' ten, and stated what kind of thing they should be:

> "이건 큰 디자인 원칙이고 각 작품별로 좀 더 구체화해서 사용할 수 있는 추상화된 기준을 만드는거야"

That request is the `user-originated` part of this page: an abstract institutional layer that each work specialises, rather than a style guide. It is the first statement on record that the user wants shared design reasoning across systems at all, which bears directly on [[Q-001-shared-design-system]].

The draft answering it is not confirmed. The conversation ends on the assistant's document with no user reply, so every principle below is `llm-proposed` and is recorded here as a working draft worth consulting, not as an adopted institutional rule.

## Draft v0.1

The draft places one sentence at its centre:

> "정교한 체계를 통해, 그 체계로 다 담을 수 없는 것을 마주하게 한다."

Each principle carries a question to judge a choice by.

1. **Good design is faithful to the work's question.** It starts from what the work handles and what it leaves the audience, not from an available form or technology. *Does this choice deepen the work's question, or only make it look plausible?*
2. **Good design speaks through experience rather than explanation.** The concept must live in what is classified, arranged, permitted, and returned — not only in an introductory text. *With the description hidden, does the core relation or tension survive?*
3. **Good design makes the system precise without reducing its subject to it.** The value or name a system assigns is not the whole of what it names, and the tension between the form and what escapes it is preserved by precision, not by sloppiness. A flawed implementation and a system's inherent limit are different things. *Is this handling its subject, or claiming to have captured it?*
4. **Good design's form comes from role.** Typeface, colour, size, spacing, and motion follow what is speaking and what must be distinguished. Legibility, balance, and rhythm are sufficient reasons; not every element needs a symbol. *What does this form distinguish or make possible?*
5. **Good design makes use clear and leaves interpretation open.** The audience should know their state, their options, and what their action changed — without being told which feeling or lesson is correct. Openness is not the same as missing information. *Is this room to interpret, or an omission?*
6. **Good design does not overstate what it can do.** Looking institutional is not grounds for implying authority, guarantees, or effects that do not exist. Internal fiction may be dense; what actually happens to the audience is handled separately. *Does what this makes them believe match what can actually be provided?*
7. **Good design keeps only necessary complexity.** Appearing sparse is not the goal. Dense screens may be dense; hiding a needed comparison to look clean is not restraint, and adding procedure to look official is not precision. *What is lost by removing this, and what does keeping it enable?*
8. **Good design makes consistency, not uniformity.** The systems need not share a look, a density, or an atmosphere. What is shared is the logic by which choices are made, so that differences are explicable within it. *Does this difference follow from the work's role, or was it decided arbitrarily?*
9. **Good design respects the audience at every touchpoint.** Small strings, empty states, errors, refusals, long records, and small screens carry the same care. An institutional register may be dry without being careless; a required absence or refusal is itself designed. *At the moments no one looks at, is the audience's time, understanding, and choice respected?*
10. **Good design concentrates on what lasts and updates the method.** Durability comes from separating lasting questions and attitudes from replaceable expression and technology. The principles are not exempt and may be revised against the work's purpose. *If the current tools and fashions change, does the reason for this choice remain?*

The draft closes on a summary line: `체계는 정교하게. 사용은 명료하게. 해석은 열어 두게.`

## The proposed layering

| Layer | Question it answers | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional principles | Why is this choice appropriate? | Judgment criteria applied across every system |
| Per-system design principles | What does the principle require here? | Audience experience, emphasis, priorities |
| Implementation guide | How is it expressed and operated? | Typeface, colour, layout, information density, interaction |

[[DEC-006-typographic-voice]] is the one worked instance on record. Its rule — that the typeface follows who is speaking — is principle 4 at institutional level; the specific typefaces are the implementation layer and may change while the reason does not.

Where principles collide, the draft proposes an order: needed information over a clean screen, the work's question over a common appearance, and clarity about real consequences over immersive ambiguity.

## What it departs from

The draft explicitly declines to carry over one premise from its sources — that a product is a tool and not an art object. Under it, function includes stopping, feeling, and judging, so what is removed is friction without reason, not the tension a work needs. This is a deliberate departure, not an omission.

## Verification limits

The two source videos are present only as Korean auto-generated transcripts inside the export, with no title, channel, upload date, or URL, and with visible transcription damage including mangled proper names. They are not registered as sources. Attributions in them to Dieter Rams, Louis Sullivan, Mies van der Rohe, Braun, and Apple are the speakers' claims and were not independently verified; quote them as what the transcripts say. The assistant is an external session with no access to this repository's records, so the draft was written without reference to [[DEC-004-institutional-voice]], [[DEC-005-public-facing-language]], or the existing open questions, and has not been checked for conflict with them.
