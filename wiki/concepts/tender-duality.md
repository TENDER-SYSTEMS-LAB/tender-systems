# Tender Duality

The institution's name carries two meanings at once. `Tender` names tenderness — softness, intimacy, vulnerability, care. `Tender` also names the commercial and legal sense of the word — an offer, a bid, a submission, and, in `legal tender`, currency itself.

The user required that this duality be recorded as part of the identity, stating it directly:

> "Tender 라는 단어도 이중적이라는 뜻을 가지고 있어. 이 내용도 추가해야해."

## Why it matters

The duality is not decoration on the name. It is what lets a single institution stand behind systems built on commerce and systems built on finance without either framing feeling like a borrowed costume. `OTHER GOODS` (commerce) and `LONGING` (finance/research) both operate through the transactional half of `tender`, while both take as their subject matter the tender — in the soft sense — material that a commercial or financial system was never built to hold. The name states the method before either system does.

## Not yet settled

In the same conversation, the assistant elaborated this duality into a fuller two-column framework (`tenderness` — softness, intimacy, vulnerability, emotion — set against `tender` — offer, bid, transaction, legal tender) and proposed several candidate slogans built on it, including:

> "A company for things that resist systems."

and

> "Systems for tender things."

These formulations are `llm-proposed`. They received no user reply in the conversation and must not be read as settled copy or official language. Only the requirement that the duality itself be part of the identity is attributable to the user; the specific phrasing built on top of it is not.
