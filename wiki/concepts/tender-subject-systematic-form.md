# Tender Subject, Systematic Form

A claimed structural method shared by the institution's systems: a subject that resists measurement, valuation, ownership, or classification is put through an institutional or computational form that was never built to hold it, and that form is executed straight — run as the form actually functions, not parodied or announced as metaphor.

## The two named instances

The source names two instances of this pattern, at the level of structure only:

- A commerce system applied to things that resist commodification. The canonical source for this system is [other-goods-lab](https://github.com/TENDER-SYSTEMS-LAB/other-goods-lab).
- A financial system applied to things that resist valuation. The canonical source for this system is [longing-lab](https://github.com/TENDER-SYSTEMS-LAB/longing-lab).

This page does not describe either system beyond that structural role. Content, catalogue detail, and product-level material for each system live only in its Lab repository.

## Status of this pattern

This pattern was named and formulated by the assistant, as a synthesis drawn from work the user had already made — it was not a claim the user stated or confirmed in the conversation. Its status is accordingly `hypothesis`, not `working` or `confirmed`.

`longing-lab` separately records a project-level page on this same shared grammar (`system-grammar.md`), developed in a different conversation and framed as `jointly-developed` at the project level. This institutional page does not restate that page's content; the Lab repository remains canonical for that account of the grammar.

## Illustrative test: an aesthetic rule and speculative pairings

In the same conversation, the assistant also coined an aesthetic rule:

> "Never illustrate tenderness with tenderness."

and generated six speculative future subject-form pairings as an illustrative test of the pattern:

- `CARE × INSURANCE`
- `MEMORY × ARCHIVE`
- `REGRET × ACCOUNTING`
- `INTIMACY × LOGISTICS`
- `GRIEF × CUSTOMER SERVICE`
- `HOPE × FORECASTING`

Both the rule and the pairings are `llm-proposed` and unconfirmed by the user. The source itself frames the pairings as explicitly non-committal — noted as not needing to all be built and not needing to be decided now, offered only as a test of whether the pattern holds. They are not candidate systems.
