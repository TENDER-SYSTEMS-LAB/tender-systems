# Academic Guidance and Publication

This note records the user's interest in developing the works into academic writing and the assistant's proposed preparation routes. It is shared research context; work-specific design and implementation remain canonical in each Lab.

## User intent and evolution

Attribution: `user-originated`.

On 2026-09-07, the user asked for university professors who could review the works, advise on artistic direction, and help develop them into papers. The requested basis was relevant prior papers or artworks and experience with web or app media, preferably in Korea.

After the assistant offered a broad candidate survey, the user singled out 강이연 and the journal written as "Leonard" in the prompt, asking how to publish there. The assistant interpreted the journal as *Leonardo*. This establishes a specific interest, not a selected supervisor, agreed collaboration, graduate-school application, or committed submission plan.

## Proposed preparation route

Attribution: `llm-proposed`; no subsequent user acceptance appears in the export.

The assistant suggested choosing one work and one question, making its core experience function, retaining production evidence, seeking advice, preparing a preliminary journal proposal, and then developing a manuscript for review. It initially favored OTHER GOODS, then allowed OTHER GOODS or LONGING in the journal-specific answer. The user selected neither a work nor a research question.

Three approaches were offered: practice-based artistic research, Research through Design, and aesthetic or critical analysis of web art. The later response favored the reported `Work Bench - Article` category. These remain alternatives, not adopted institutional methods.

Proposed materials were a focused 5–8-page consultation packet and a 1–2-page English preliminary journal proposal. Both lengths were assistant suggestions rather than official requirements. Materials would connect the work's question, implementation state, screens or a short demonstration, unresolved problems, and the advice sought.

The suggested evidence chain is initial intention → implemented version → discovered problem → reason for revision → remaining limitation. This develops the process-recording interest in [[creative-origin-and-motivation]] without replacing that background motivation with a formally adopted research method.

The assistant distinguished explanations of design choices from claims about audience interpretation or changes in values, which need corresponding evidence. It also distinguished AI suggestions from user decisions, fictional data inside a work from empirical research data, and LLM criticism from actual audience responses. Separate records of AI use in production, runtime behavior, and manuscript preparation were proposed.

## Publication and identity questions

The publication instructions are a dated assistant report, not independently verified journal guidance. Detailed word limits, templates, submission routes, review models, AI policy, fees, and timing remain in raw and require checking against the journal and publisher before use. The source itself noted conflicting review-time estimates and did not treat a preliminary proposal as a guarantee of acceptance.

Unresolved questions include:

- Which work, question, research approach, and manuscript category to pursue.
- Whether a candidate can provide a one-off review, sustained guidance, or collaboration, and under what conditions.
- What is implemented and what evidence supports the eventual claims.
- How research writing may explain the work while preserving the boundary in [[Q-002-internal-versus-public-language]] and [[DEC-005-public-facing-language]].
- How author naming, affiliation, and any pseudonym relate to [[DEC-003-public-identity]]; asking the editor was proposed, but no resolution is recorded.
- What consent, ethics review, and disclosure would be needed for any participant research, and what current journal requirements apply.

No outreach, editorial contact, manuscript, submission, acceptance, or audience study is evidenced by this source. The research-writing proposal does not supersede existing public-language or institutional-identity decisions.
