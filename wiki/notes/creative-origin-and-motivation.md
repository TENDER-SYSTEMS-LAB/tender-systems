# Creative Origin and Motivation

This note preserves a recollection of inspiration and continuing motivation across works. Recorded on 2026-09-06, it is a memory aid for a future occasion to explain how the work began. It does not define an artistic philosophy or an institutional principle.

## The thought as recalled

The surrounding conversation about AI seemed focused on productive uses: automating work, earning money, or teaching others how to earn money with it.

That impression raised a question. People spend considerable money on books, performances, play, and travel, and proudly share those experiences. Why did accounts of using AI for creation that earns no money, or simply for an experience to consume and enjoy, seem so much less visible? Was it uninteresting to other people? Why did it feel as though hardly anyone was trying or sharing it?

The attraction was that this possibility felt interesting and fun. Using AI to make or experience something could itself be a reason to do it, without needing that experience to produce income or automate a task.

The user describes this thought as both an initial inspiration and a continuing source of motivation, gradually taking shape from the first inspiration and the start of work through the present. The account supplies no earlier dates or separate sequence of stages.

## Evolution

### 2026-09-06 — Engineering background and artistic possibility

A follow-up adds the technical background behind this curiosity. The user describes studying software development from a computer science/engineering background, working as a DevOps engineer, and identifying with those making advanced use of LLMs.

Could someone with that background spending tokens and money on strongly conceptual art itself be interesting to other people? Might there be an audience or demand for it? Could this way of working offer an opportunity to realize an artistic disposition that was already present? These are the possibilities the user is considering, rather than established outcomes.

The user also finds it surprising that so few people seem to be working in this way. This continues the earlier impression about what is visible, rather than establishing how many such practitioners exist.

The follow-up extends the original enjoyment and curiosity with the meeting of a technical background and artistic practice, possible interest from others, and the possibility of artistic self-realization. It does not replace the earlier motivation or set an income goal. See [[SRC-2026-09-06-engineering-background-and-artistic-possibility]].

### 2026-09-06 — A traceable creative process

Another follow-up considers the recording process itself. The user describes using GitHub to store a Wiki organized from work records and conversations with AI, with the aim of making the work logs and decision-making process fully traceable. This way of retaining the development of the work feels different from other creative processes the user has encountered.

Could the ability to follow that process itself be an interesting feature of the work's world? The interest here includes being able to retrace how the work developed through activity, dialogue, and decisions. This adds a reflection on the creative process to the earlier enjoyment, technical background, and artistic possibilities. It remains a possibility being considered, rather than a decision that the archive constitutes part of the artwork. See [[SRC-2026-09-06-traceable-creative-process]].

## How to use this record

Retain the question and the enjoyment behind it when recalling the work's beginnings. The impression about what others share is personal context, not evidence that noncommercial AI creation is absent or that this work pioneered it. This account does not establish an objection to productive or commercial uses of AI.

The requested role is background memory. It is not an adopted creative rule, a public statement, or a resolution of the separate public-language question. The Korean sources retain the original wording; this page is their English synthesis. These sources are direct personal accounts from the same conversation. They preserve the motivation and its development, not independent evidence of audience demand, rarity, or leadership in LLM use. The wish to make the whole process traceable does not verify that every conversation or decision has already been captured and uploaded.
