# Creative Origin and Motivation

This note preserves a recollection of inspiration and continuing motivation across works. Recorded on 2026-09-06, it is a memory aid for a future occasion to explain how the work began. It does not define an artistic philosophy or an institutional principle.

## The thought as recalled

The surrounding conversation about AI seemed focused on productive uses: automating work, earning money, or teaching others how to earn money with it.

That impression raised a question. People spend considerable money on books, performances, play, and travel, and proudly share those experiences. Why did accounts of using AI for creation that earns no money, or simply for an experience to consume and enjoy, seem so much less visible? Was it uninteresting to other people? Why did it feel as though hardly anyone was trying or sharing it?

The attraction was that this possibility felt interesting and fun. Using AI to make or experience something could itself be a reason to do it, without needing that experience to produce income or automate a task.

The user describes this thought as both an initial inspiration and a continuing source of motivation, gradually taking shape from the first inspiration and the start of work through the present. The account supplies no earlier dates or separate sequence of stages.

## Evolution

### 2026-09-06 — Engineering background and artistic possibility

A follow-up adds the technical background behind this curiosity. The user describes studying software development from a computer science/engineering background, working as a DevOps engineer, and identifying with those making advanced use of LLMs.

Could someone with that background spending tokens and money on strongly conceptual art itself be interesting to other people? Might there be an audience or demand for it? Could this way of working offer an opportunity to realize an artistic disposition that was already present? These are the possibilities the user is considering, rather than established outcomes.

The user also finds it surprising that so few people seem to be working in this way. This continues the earlier impression about what is visible, rather than establishing how many such practitioners exist.

The follow-up extends the original enjoyment and curiosity with the meeting of a technical background and artistic practice, possible interest from others, and the possibility of artistic self-realization. It does not replace the earlier motivation or set an income goal. See [[SRC-2026-09-06-engineering-background-and-artistic-possibility]].

### 2026-09-06 — A traceable creative process

Another follow-up considers the recording process itself. The user describes using GitHub to store a Wiki organized from work records and conversations with AI, with the aim of making the work logs and decision-making process fully traceable. This way of retaining the development of the work feels different from other creative processes the user has encountered.

Could the ability to follow that process itself be an interesting feature of the work's world? The interest here includes being able to retrace how the work developed through activity, dialogue, and decisions. This adds a reflection on the creative process to the earlier enjoyment, technical background, and artistic possibilities. It remains a possibility being considered, rather than a decision that the archive constitutes part of the artwork. See [[SRC-2026-09-06-traceable-creative-process]].

### 2026-09-07 — Three motivations stated after an external video

Watching an external video about AI companies prompted the user to write three further statements of current creative motivation. They add to this background record. They do not replace the earlier motivation, and recording them does not adopt them as an artistic philosophy, an institutional principle, or public copy.

#### What the user stated

The Korean original is the evidence; the English below renders it. See [[SRC-2026-09-07-creator-motivation-statements]].

1. **Making work with a medium the user has misgivings about.** Using AI — which *could* become a threat to people and *could* cause romance to disappear — to make work on the subject of that disappearing human romance. The possibility wording is the user's own. It is not a prediction that AI will remove humanity, not a refusal to use AI, and not a legal or political position on any company.
2. **Spending the user's own money to work as an artist.** In a world where money and power lead people to compromise or abandon living by their own values, the user "wastes" their own money and makes work for their self-realization as an artist. `'허비'`, kept in quotation marks, is the user's word. It is not restated as investment or as a success strategy. It names no amount, no permanent refusal of income, and no claim that spending money makes work more genuine.
3. **What the user hopes the work opens up.** That through the work it becomes possible to discover, reflect on, and empathize with things such as trust, love, and understanding. This is a motivation in its own right, not a footnote to the first two. The statement does not say whether the one having that experience is the maker, the audience, or both, and this record does not narrow it.

`낭만` is kept in Korean because the institution has no agreed English term for it. In this sense it is not confined to romantic love, and no definition is settled here. The relationship between `낭만` and trust, love, and understanding is not stated either.

#### Interpretation, not statement

The following is an assistant reading offered for review, `llm-proposed`. It is not what the user said and has not been accepted.

- The first statement can be read as work made in conscious contradiction rather than work that resolves one: the medium is neither an enemy outside the work nor a neutral tool. It does not follow that the contradiction is already a settled concept for a work, nor that a good subject offsets any question about AI use.
- The second can be read as a motive for making, not as a rule for how any work operates. Spending the user's own money says nothing about pricing, currency, payment, or economics inside a system.
- The third can be extended toward audience experience — room for a viewer to discover, reflect, and empathize — but warmth of motive does not imply warm surfaces, sentimental copy, or explanatory text in a work. [[DEC-005-public-facing-language]] still governs public copy.

Reinforced: the earlier account of AI creation pursued for enjoyment and artistic self-realization rather than income, and the note's standing role as background rather than philosophy.

Added: an explicit subject for the work, an explicit reason for bearing its cost, and an explicit hope for what the work opens up.

Still open: whether these motivations govern any particular system, how `낭만` relates to trust, love, and understanding, and whether the experience in the third statement belongs to the maker, the audience, or both. ## How to use this record

Retain the question and the enjoyment behind it when recalling the work's beginnings. The impression about what others share is personal context, not evidence that noncommercial AI creation is absent or that this work pioneered it. This account does not establish an objection to productive or commercial uses of AI.

The requested role is background memory. It is not an adopted creative rule, a public statement, or a resolution of the separate public-language question. The Korean sources retain the original wording; this page is their English synthesis. The user's sources are direct personal accounts. They preserve the motivation and its development, not independent evidence of audience demand, rarity, or leadership in LLM use. The 2026-09-07 statements are the user's; the video registered beside them is external material that occasioned them and carries no verified claim into this page. The wish to make the whole process traceable does not verify that every conversation or decision has already been captured and uploaded.
