# Q-004 — Words That Shape Judgment

## Question

The user asked how the works could reflect a familiar effect: people's judgments are shaped by the words they have just seen or heard.

> "사람들은 눈으로 보거나 들은 단어들이 어떤 것을 판단하는데 영향을 받는 효과를 해당 작품 세계관에 반영해본다고 한다면 어떤식으로 적용해볼 수 있을까?"

The question is `user-originated`. Everything below the question is `llm-proposed`. The user asked for it to be recorded and did not accept or reject any part of it.

## Why it matters

Both active systems already attach words to things. LONGING RESEARCH rates, targets, and codes. OTHER GOODS names, prices, and shelves. If a system's word makes value rather than describing it, the effect sits at the centre of [[tender-subject-systematic-form]]: a precise form applied to something it cannot fully hold. It also sits beside [[DEC-006-typographic-voice]]. The user now grounds that rule in the idea that a typeface is its speaker's persona, so typeface is itself one channel for this effect. The word sets *what* is judged; the typeface sets *whose* judgment it is.

## Which effects are usable

The assistant's assessment of the evidence, not independently verified:

| Effect | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Anchoring — a number seen first becomes the reference | Strong |
| Framing — the same fact judged differently by its wording | Strong |
| Labeling and naming — a name or class sets meaning | Moderate to strong |
| Repetition (illusory truth) — repeated statements feel truer | Strong |
| Behavioural priming — a word changes later action | Weak; many failed replications |

The proposal is not to make behavioural priming a premise of any work. Claiming it would overstate what the work can do, which draft principle 6 in [[design-principles]] warns against.

## Candidate applications

These are listed here only while unadopted. Once the user adopts one, its detail belongs in the owning Lab, per [[DEC-002-decision-placement]].

### LN-001 LONGING RESEARCH

1. **Anchoring.** Analyst target prices shown before the viewer records a position become anchors. One edition could show them before the viewer's judgment and another after.
2. **Framing.** Two analysts read the same week's data in opposite frames, with waiting as a cost versus waiting as a yield. The viewer leans toward whichever report was read first.
3. **Labeling.** `NO COVERAGE`, `UNCLASSIFIED`, and `Unexplained` already exist. One possible world rule is that initiating coverage moves a price, so being named has a price effect. Its fit with the Lab's world rules is unchecked.
4. **Headline verbs.** The verb chosen for the same event ("collapses" or "eases") sets the reading before the price line. This depends on the Lab's undecided speaker for the event layer.
5. **Turning it back on the viewer.** This is the assistant's strongest proposal. The system records which frames and anchors each viewer met. It later breaks down that viewer's own position by cause in the form of the attribution ledger, leaving an `Unexplained` remainder. The viewer notices their judgment moved without being told why.

### OG-001 OTHER GOODS

1. **Names as frames.** "First Canceled Order" and "Order #4471, Canceled" are one object read two ways. This is the Lab's open naming-register question in another form. The same object could be offered under two names.
2. **Price as an anchor of meaning.** A price makes "Unit 12B, Unoccupied" a commodity. A strikethrough "was" price on a sentimental item shows a discounted longing.
3. **Category labels.** A "Recently Lost" shelf and a "Clearance" shelf frame the same item differently. The Lab's ontology work is effectively designing this effect.
4. **Status words.** In the records-reading first edition, `Delivered`, `Returned to sender`, and `Unclaimed` set the reading before the record is read.

## Proposed boundaries

- **Not explained on the surface.** Naming the effect where the audience can see it would cancel the effect and would break [[DEC-005-public-facing-language]].
- **Interpretation, not consequence.** Moving how the audience reads something is the work. Steering real payment or action through a false impression is a dark pattern. This matters most for OTHER GOODS, where transactions may be real. The line runs through draft principle 6 and that Lab's open question of whether it sells a product or a transaction.

## Open points

- Whether the user wants the effect reflected at all, and in which work first.
- Whether it is a cross-system principle or a device chosen work by work. If it is a principle, it is a candidate for [[design-principles]].
- Whether an audience-facing measurement such as candidate 5 is wanted. Recording what each viewer saw is a data practice, and it needs a disclosure decision before it exists.
- The evidence table is the assistant's summary and was not checked against literature during this ingestion.
