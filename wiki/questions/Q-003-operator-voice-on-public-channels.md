# Q-003 — Operator Voice on Public Channels

## Question

Whether long-form commentary spoken by the operator in the first person, explaining how and why the work was made, falls under the public-language rule, and under whose identity such a channel would run.

## Why it matters

[[DEC-005-public-facing-language]] holds that public copy states what a system does and never explains, interprets, or labels the work; its stated consequences name bios, profile text, repository descriptions, and README copy. [[DEC-003-public-identity]] holds that public surfaces carry no personal attribution. A channel whose premise is the operator explaining decisions in the first person tests both rules at once, and neither rule was written with that surface in mind.

## What the source contains

On 2026-09-08 the user listed four video topics and asked for further recommendations:

> "1. 나는 누구이고 뭘 하는 사람인지
> 2. 왜 아직 작품에 로고가 없는지
> 3. 왜 글꼴은 이렇게 했는지
> 4. 작품을 AI와 만들면서 든 생각들 - 내가 병목이 됨"

Three of the four are bare topic names. Only the fourth carries a claim, about the operator's own role in the work:

> "작품을 AI와 만들면서 든 생각들 - 내가 병목이 됨"

In a later message the user derived two topic titles from candidates the assistant had proposed:

> "직장인인 내가 작품을 만드는 시간 에서 보고 든 컨텐츠는 "본업이 따로 있나요?" 의 주제로 하나 할 수 있을 것 같아.
> 왜 작품을 회사처럼 만들고 있나 에서 든 컨텐츠는 "작품을 시작하게 된 그 시점의 경험(새벽에 갑자기 영감이 떠오름)""

The same message stated a cadence preference:

> "한주에 하나씩 영상을 공개하면 좋을 것 같네."

From these inputs the assistant produced a wide topic menu grouped by theme, a specific ordering for an initial run of episodes, a five-step video structure, a closing framing describing the channel's stance toward its own incompleteness, and a channel name built on the operator's given name. The conversation ends on that compiled document, with no user reply to any of it.

## The tension

Two readings are possible, and the conversation resolves neither.

- **Reading one:** several proposed episodes are exactly what the public-language rule excludes — among them a video on why the work is not called an art work, and a recurring series explaining design decisions such as the absent logo and the chosen typeface. On this reading the plan contradicts a settled rule.
- **Reading two:** that rule's stated consequences name static surface copy — bios, profile text, repository descriptions, README copy — not long-form video commentary, so this surface may simply fall outside a scope the rule never defined. [[Q-002-internal-versus-public-language]] already records an adjacent undefined boundary between internal and public registers.

The conversation does not choose between these readings, because the assistant had no access to these records and the user did not raise them.

## Identity

The channel as described speaks in the operator's first person, and the proposed name is built on the operator's given name. Neither party in the conversation acknowledges any conflict with [[DEC-003-public-identity]]. It is also undecided whether such a channel would belong to the institution, to a system, or to the operator personally — it matches none of the three per-entity account structures recorded in [[public-surfaces]]. The proposed name itself is deliberately not reproduced in this Wiki.

## Open sub-questions

- Whether the public-language rule in [[DEC-005-public-facing-language]] covers operator-voiced video at all.
- Whether a personally voiced surface may exist alongside the institutional and per-system surfaces, and under whose account it would run.
- Whether background material already held as private memory (see [[creative-origin-and-motivation]]) may be published as public content through such a channel.
- Whether an answer here also settles [[Q-002-internal-versus-public-language]].
