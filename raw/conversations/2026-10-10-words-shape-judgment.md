## Turn 2 — assistant (condensed)

**Premise.** Both works are institutions that attach words to things: ratings, names, classifications. Reflecting the effect means letting the audience experience that the system's word makes value rather than only describing it.

**Effects and how well supported they are.**
- Anchoring: strong.
- Framing: strong.
- Labeling and naming: moderate to strong.
- Repetition (the illusory truth effect): strong.
- Behavioural priming, where a single word changes later action: weak, with many failed replications. The assistant advised against making it a premise of the works.

**LONGING RESEARCH candidates.**
1. **Anchoring.** Showing analyst target prices before the viewer records a position makes them anchors. An edition that shows them before judgment could be compared with one that shows them after.
2. **Framing.** Two analysts read the same weekly data in opposite frames, with waiting as a cost versus waiting as a yield. The viewer's position leans toward whichever report was read first.
3. **Labeling.** `NO COVERAGE`, `UNCLASSIFIED`, and `Unexplained` already exist in the work. A possible world rule is that initiating coverage, and so being named, moves a price. Its fit with the existing world rules is unchecked.
4. **Headline verbs.** The verb used for the same event ("collapses" or "eases") shapes judgment before the price line is read. This depends on the undecided speaker of the event layer.
5. **Turning it back on the viewer (strongest proposal).** The system records which frames and anchors each viewer saw. It later breaks that viewer's own position down by cause, in the form of the attribution ledger, with an `Unexplained` remainder. The viewer notices their judgment moved without being told why.

**OTHER GOODS candidates.**
1. **Product names as frames.** "First Canceled Order" and "Order #4471, Canceled" are the same object, one sad and one administrative. This connects to the naming-register question. The same object could be offered under two names.
2. **Price as an anchor of meaning.** A price turns "Unit 12B, Unoccupied" into a commodity. A strikethrough "was" price on a sentimental item shows a discounted longing.
3. **Category labels.** The same item reads differently on a "Recently Lost" shelf than on a "Clearance" shelf. The ontology work is effectively designing this effect.
4. **Status words.** In the records-reading first edition, `Delivered`, `Returned to sender`, and `Unclaimed` decide the reading first.

**Link to typeface.** Typeface is also a channel for this effect. The same sentence reads as fact in Inconsolata and as opinion in Source Serif. The word sets what is judged; the typeface sets whose judgment it is.

**Boundaries.**
- Do not explain the effect on the surface. Doing so cancels it and conflicts with DEC-005.
- Moving interpretation is the work. Steering real payment or action through a false impression is a dark pattern. This line runs through draft design principle 6 and the OTHER GOODS question of product or transaction.
